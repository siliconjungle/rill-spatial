# Spatial relevance at 20,000 connected observers

Research date: 2026-10-20. Starting revision: `ef1fbbc44cfcd62c54ec7d6ab3e1892d734f15a1`. This is research and a proposed measurement plan, not a demonstrated server capacity result. The current comparison screens 52 competitor entries from 38 projects; its strict acceptance gate remains OPEN. The machine available for exploration is an Apple M2 with 8 GB RAM and approximately 23 GiB free disk, below the user's 100 GB reserve. No builds, dependency copies, GPU experiments, or server load tests were run for this document. Only useful research artifacts are retained.

## What has to scale

Separate four quantities: connected observers `N`, indexed entities `M`, average exact matches per observer `K`, and changed visible records per replication interval `C`. Twenty thousand idle connections, twenty thousand radius queries, and twenty thousand actively receiving clients are different tests. With `N=M=20,000`, naive directed all-to-all examines 400 million pairs per relevance tick, or 8 billion at 20 Hz. Excluding each observer's own entity changes that to 399,980,000, not the order of growth. The existing API includes coincident/self points when they match.

For a bounded-density workload with stable average `K`, useful reporting work is proportional to `N K`. This is an output lower bound, not a guaranteed total complexity bound for any index: construction, updates, traversal, false candidates, sorting, allocations and pathological distributions still matter. A grid can degenerate in a crowded cell; a tree can lose pruning with large radii or overlap. Any API materializing every ID must write every returned ID. Shared traversal can reduce search work, but cannot make 400 million public `u64` results disappear. Range counting or a shared result handle can avoid expansion only under a separately specified output contract. The distinction follows the all-pairs and spatial-join literature ([Gray and Moore](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2000/file/7385db9a3f11415bc0e9e2625fae3734-Paper.pdf), [Curtin et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/curtin13.html), [Brinkhoff et al.](https://sigmodrecord.org/1993/06/03/efficient-processing-of-spatial-joins-using-r-trees/)).

The table uses decimal MB/GB and assumes one result row per observer. `u32` is a possible dense internal/wire index only; the public library returns arbitrary stable `u64` IDs. A wire remapping needs a generation/lifetime protocol and must not narrow the public ID contract.

| Mean K | Directed matches/tick | Materialized u32 IDs/tick | Public u64 IDs/tick | u64 ID writes at 20 Hz | Payload egress at 32 bytes/match, 20 Hz |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32 | 640,000 | 2.56 MB | 5.12 MB | 102.4 MB/s | 409.6 MB/s = 3.28 Gbit/s |
| 128 | 2,560,000 | 10.24 MB | 20.48 MB | 409.6 MB/s | 1.6384 GB/s = 13.11 Gbit/s |
| 512 | 10,240,000 | 40.96 MB | 81.92 MB | 1.6384 GB/s | 6.5536 GB/s = 52.43 Gbit/s |
| 19,999 | 399,980,000 | 1.59992 GB | 3.19984 GB | 63.9968 GB/s | 255.9872 GB/s = 2.048 Tbit/s |

These are arithmetic scenarios, not measured transport requirements: `B_payload = N K f S`. They exclude packet headers, acks, encryption, loss recovery, initial spawns, removals, queues and administrative traffic. At `K=128`, `S=32`, `f=20`, each client receives 81,920 bytes/s (655.36 kbit/s). At `K=19,999` it receives about 12.80 MB/s (102.39 Mbit/s). A 10% changed-record fraction reduces the `K=128` steady-state payload to 163.84 MB/s (1.31 Gbit/s), only if unchanged records need no payload and baseline/visibility churn remains small. A simultaneous join or teleport can invalidate that favorable fraction. Delta encoding requires acknowledged baselines and bootstrap handling ([Fiedler](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_compression/)).

At `K=128`, current and previous public ID lists alone occupy about 40.96 MB, before capacity slack and membership structures. A CSR-style layout adds approximately 160 KB for 20,001 64-bit offsets; 20,000 separate Rust Vec headers add approximately 480 KB on a typical 64-bit target. A 20,000×20,000 one-bit relation occupies 50 MB per snapshot, but always reserves quadratic space and needs ID-slot lifetime handling. Eight 32-byte baseline records for each of 2.56 million visible pairs use 655.36 MB; baseline design can matter more than the index. Reserving 64 KiB per connection costs 1.31 GB for one queue direction; two such directions cost 2.62 GB. Actual socket memory, sharing and baseline retention must be measured rather than inferred from these examples.

## Deadlines and a testable resource envelope

At 60 Hz simulation the frame deadline is 16.667 ms; 20 Hz relevance gives a 50 ms period. A synchronous relevance burst still has to fit the simulation frame in which it runs. A 50 ms relevance period does not grant permission to stall simulation for 50 ms. Alternatives are (a) a separate bounded compute phase reading an immutable epoch, or (b) three cohorts of about 6,667 observers per simulation frame. Cohorts reduce bursts but introduce phase-dependent age; comparing them with a single epoch requires explicit timestamp semantics. Unity documents distinct network/simulation tick rates and cohort-like spreading of snapshot work ([ghost snapshots](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.netcode%401.5/manual/ghost-snapshots.html)).

Use the following as candidate envelopes for future provisioning tests, not hardware recommendations or capacity promises:

| Envelope | Explicit assumptions | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Existing M2 development machine | 8 GB unified RAM; shared workers; insufficient free-disk reserve | Small correctness proofs and exploratory counters; no isolated-host or 20k-server claim |
| One future server | 16–32 physical cores, 64–128 GiB RAM, 25 Gbit/s link; initially cap sustained application egress to 60% of line rate | A bounded-K workload could fit the link arithmetic; full simulation, CPU, packet rate and tails remain unproven |
| Regional/sharded deployment | Several such hosts; one authority per entity; explicit migration and halo exchange | Distribution of ownership and replication cost, including cross-shard hotspots; cannot eliminate output required by every observer |

For the one-server example, 60% of 25 Gbit/s is 15 Gbit/s payload budget: `K=128, S=32, 20 Hz` already consumes 13.11 Gbit/s before overhead. It has little headroom for bootstrap or churn. At an assumed 1,200 useful bytes/datagram, that scenario requires at least about 1.365 million datagrams/s; 400,000 observer snapshots/s alone is not the packet count when snapshots span datagrams. A 10 Gbit/s link has an ideal raw-payload ceiling `K≈97.7` under these assumptions, or `K≈58.6` at the same 60% budget, before overhead. Host speed does not change these bounds. RSS/RFS/XPS and NIC queues affect packet processing locality but are Linux-specific and require separate server tests ([kernel networking guide](https://docs.kernel.org/networking/scaling.html)).

An initial admission target could reserve <=5 ms p99 of each simulation frame for a relevance cohort and <=40 ms p99 from its scheduled start to ready-to-send work, with a 50 ms hard relevance deadline and <=100 ms p99 replica age for the 20 Hz tier under a declared network profile. These are proposed engineering gates, not user requirements or achieved results. Record p99.9, maximum, deadline misses and late work rather than hiding them behind p99. Reserve CPU for simulation, ingress, encryption and egress; test fixed compute-pool sizes and total runnable threads. Tokio specifically cautions that CPU work needs bounded concurrency; Rayon permits an explicit thread count ([Tokio](https://docs.rs/tokio/latest/tokio/task/fn.spawn_blocking.html), [Rayon](https://docs.rs/rayon/latest/rayon/struct.ThreadPoolBuilder.html)).

## Mechanisms worth implementing

### Shared exact search

**Persistent cell read view with exact leaf filtering.** Store occupied-cell descriptors and contiguous point/ID arrays; group observers by cell or Morton order; share conservative candidate-cell lists where possible. Reuse buffers and fuse gather/filter/write. Cell size is tuned, not a global query-radius restriction. Neighbor-cell-only AOI, as documented by Mirror, is useful production evidence for locality but does not implement arbitrary exact Euclidean radius membership. Every candidate must still pass the existing f64 predicate unless a conservatively proved bound accepts a whole cell. Avoid adding a persistent grid alongside a packed tree unless its measured benefit pays for update and memory costs ([Mirror](https://mirror-networking.gitbook.io/docs/manual/interest-management/spatial-hashing), [PostGIS bounding-box filter plus distance](https://postgis.net/docs/manual-dev/en/ST_DWithin.html)).

**Observer/object dual tree.** Build a hierarchy over query centers as well as points. Reject query-node/object-node pairs when their conservative minimum distance exceeds every radius in the query node; accept a Cartesian block only when its conservative maximum distance is within every radius. With radius extrema, the corresponding real-geometry conditions are `d_min > r_max` for reject and `d_max <= r_min` for accept. Overlapping radius distributions weaken pruning; grouping similar radii can help. These are candidate rules requiring a floating-point proof against the library's ordered f64/scaled-hypot oracle. If bounds are uncertain, descend or run the scalar predicate. SciPy's all-pairs query documents the dual-tree use case; its approximate epsilon mode is a different contract ([dual-tree framework](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/curtin13.html), [query_ball_tree](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.spatial.cKDTree.query_ball_tree.html)).

**Coherent independent traversal is a competing combined variant.** Query ordering and packed leaves may improve cache locality with less control-flow overhead than dual-tree traversal. Karras's GPU traversal analysis demonstrates the tradeoff between reduced geometric work and execution divergence; it is motivation, not evidence of a Rust CPU win. Preserve arbitrary query centers, per-query radii and row identities after sorting ([Karras traversal](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/thinking-parallel-part-ii-tree-traversal-gpu/), [SciPy batched query_ball_point](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.spatial.cKDTree.query_ball_point.html)).

**Dense-output route.** Whole-node acceptance, precomputed contiguous ID slices and bulk copying may outperform elaborate pruning when almost everything matches. Compare public `u64` materialization, callback streaming, count-only and compact/shared results separately. The latter three can be useful APIs, but none can substitute for the public report benchmark. A retained all-visible fixture detects whether a clever search simply moves cost into expansion.

### Temporal coherence without stale answers

**Refit, dirty leaves and measured rebuild.** Batch mutations, update conservative bounds and perform queries at an explicit committed epoch. Compare refit-only against periodic rebuild and a threshold based on node/leaf visits or overlap quality. PhysX uses a main tree, a secondary structure for new objects and incremental replacement, and warns that refitting topology for too long can degrade queries. Box2D exposes tree statistics, enlargement and partial rebuilds. These production mechanisms transfer well; their exact APIs and floating types do not establish this library's f64 behavior ([PhysX](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/docs/SceneQueries.html), [Box2D](https://box2d.org/documentation/group__tree.html), [Catto](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_DynamicBVH_Full.pdf)).

**Standing query cuts or cached candidate supersets.** Preserve where a previous tree traversal stopped, then invalidate/revisit when observer position/radius, entities, bounds, structure or ID generations change. Sirikata shows standing cuts and conservative aggregation of nearby queries; its visible-size selection and object aggregation also change semantics. For exact radius reporting, a buffered candidate superset can remain correct if its displacement/radius bound is proved and every current result is re-filtered. Skipping work merely because a cell membership did not change is unsafe: objects can cross a radius boundary inside the same cell. Teleports and radius increases require immediate invalidation ([Sirikata](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/atc12/atc12-final18.pdf)).

**Static/dynamic split.** Share a long-lived static index and rebuild/refit the dynamic subset. Merge disjoint ID results without duplicates. This pays only when movement is sparse and transitions between sets are correct; include all-moving and frequent static/dynamic transitions. Sirikata's static-heavy observed world cannot justify assuming static-heavy player entities. The paper's specific movement percentages and rebuild intervals are not transferred as tuning defaults.

### Replication work sharing and bounded service

Compute the exact relevance set independently from the send policy. Diff current/previous membership to obtain enter/leave events, serialize common object state once per epoch/representation, and share that immutable encoded state among connection owners. Per-client baselines, authorization and envelopes remain connection-specific. Lossless delta coding can preserve the state representation while lowering payload; lowering frequency, quantizing values, top-K selection, hysteresis, solid-angle selection or aggregate impostors changes temporal/spatial/fidelity semantics and belongs to a separately declared server tier. Engine systems demonstrate persistent shared lists, filtering and prioritization, not a proof of 20k clients ([Replication Graph](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/replication-graph-in-unreal-engine), [Iris filtering](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/iris-filtering-in-unreal-engine), [Iris prioritization](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/iris-prioritization-in-unreal-engine), [Unity optimizations](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.netcode%401.5/manual/optimizations.html)).

Use explicit per-connection and global byte, packet, pending-spawn, query-work and queue budgets. Schedule with age-aware fairness so a busy client does not starve quiet clients and low-priority objects have a declared maximum age where feasible. Critical ownership/removal/control messages need reserved service; a crowd should not indefinitely retain deleted objects. Bound baseline memory and handle resync after loss. Bound bursty initial joins and teleports. Coalesce superseded state only where latest-state delivery is the protocol; reliable events need distinct handling. UDP itself supplies no congestion control ([RFC 8085](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8085/)).

Radius membership is a broad eligibility test, not authorization: apply server-owned team/visibility/ownership rules before exposing data. The generic exact library should continue accepting its specified finite inputs; an internet-facing adapter can validate caller entitlements, rate-limit untrusted queries, constrain requested radii or reject an unaffordable operation explicitly. Never silently truncate an exact result. If all-to-all cannot fit, the application must select and expose a capacity/fidelity policy, spread the world across regions/instances, or reject admission. Changing a search algorithm alone does not resolve that product decision. Historical interest-management comparisons and Colyseus motivate relevance filtering and distributed authority, but evaluate different worlds and consistency assumptions ([Boulanger](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~jboula2/thesis.pdf), [Colyseus](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/nsdi06/tech/full_papers/bharambe/bharambe_html/)).

### Multicore and GPU boundaries

Prefer one owner for mutable entity state and one owner for each connection/baseline/egress queue. Share a read-only index epoch for chunked query work, return compact per-owner batches and avoid one task or lock per visible edge. Spatial shards need conservative boundary/halo coverage, migration epochs and a dedup rule; splitting a crowd across owners does not reduce a client's visible output. Seastar's explicit ownership/message-passing model and read-copy-update are useful references; neither implies a free Rust integration. A simple double-buffered epoch with bounded reader lifetime may be sufficient and should be measured before introducing reclamation machinery ([Seastar](https://seastar.io/shared-nothing/), [userspace RCU](https://liburcu.org/)).

GPU work is lower priority for the current exact f64 API. Broad-phase grids, radix/BVH construction, coherent traversal and scan/compaction are relevant building blocks ([GPU broad phase](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-v-physics-simulation/chapter-32-broad-phase-collision-detection-cuda), [Karras construction](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/parallelforall/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/karras2012hpg_paper.pdf), [scan](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-vi-gpu-computing/chapter-39-parallel-prefix-sum-scan-cuda)). Time submission, data preparation, candidate generation, compaction, completion wait, CPU exact filtering and final materialization together. WebGPU mapping waits for GPU use to finish; Apple shared memory still requires CPU/GPU ordering. Unified memory does not erase synchronization or shared-memory bandwidth costs ([WebGPU](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/), [Metal shared storage](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlstoragemode/shared)).

WGSL's concrete scalar floating types are f32/f16, not the library's f64. A GPU f32 broad phase must conservatively contain every f64 match over the declared domain, then use the CPU oracle; naive conversion can lose boundary matches or overflow. If a conservative conversion proof is unavailable, restrict and label the supported domain or fall back to CPU. GPU-only f32 membership is a changed contract. Avoid constructing an `N×M` distance matrix. Use bounded candidate/output buffers, explicit overflow handling and a CPU fallback ([WGSL specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/)).

## Ranked experiments and acceptance

Run variants as combinations with their preparation and output cost included. Keep isolated mechanism counters as diagnostics, not final performance claims.

| Rank / ID | Combined variant | Main hypothesis | Required comparison / limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 / E1 | Current scalar/SIMD packed baseline + buffer reuse + full output accounting | Establish cost by K, not observer count alone | Update + prepare + query + materialization; u64 report is primary; counters/checksums do not replace reports |
| 2 / E2 | Packed cell read view + observer cell/Morton ordering + shared candidate lists + exact f64 filter | Nearby queries reuse data/traversal and avoid hash lookups | Include sorting/build/update cost, mixed radii, sparse and crowded cells; restore original query row identity |
| 3 / E3 | Observer/object dual tree + radius grouping + Cartesian block acceptance + contiguous u64 output | Share pruning across batches while preserving arbitrary centers/radii | Compare coherent independent traversal; all-visible still writes all IDs; prove conservative f64 bounds |
| 4 / E4 | Best E2/E3 + dirty refit/static-dynamic split + adaptive rebuild | Temporal coherence reduces rebuild cost without stale results | Movement 0/1/10/100%, adversarial crossings, teleport waves, insert/remove churn; measure worst rebuild frame |
| 5 / E5 | Best exact batch + membership delta + shared object serialization + per-owner baselines + fair byte/packet queues | End-to-end replication cost falls without changing exact eligibility | Validate reconstructed state under loss/reordering, bootstrap and churn; report changed fraction and queue age |
| 6 / E6 | Best E4/E5 + bounded fixed compute pool + immutable epoch + connection ownership | Coarse batches scale without starving simulation/network | 1/2/4/... physical cores; include queue wait, epoch memory and handoff bytes; no oversubscription claims |
| 7 / E7 | Best CPU path + standing candidate superset/query cuts | Incremental query work scales with motion/change | Full invalidation proof; stale-cache adversarial tests; track memory per standing query |
| 8 / E8 | Conservative GPU broad phase + scan/compact + CPU f64 filter + bounded output | Large coherent batches amortize submission and completion | Compare total scheduled-to-ready latency and memory against best CPU; GPU-domain proof and overflow fallback required |
| Separate / F1 | Exact eligibility + distance/age tiers, quantized/delta state, LOD aggregates or admission caps | Fit declared replication fidelity and egress envelope | Different server policy; publish max age/error/visibility guarantees; never count as an exact-query win |

Use fixed seeds and a held-out seed for 2D and 3D with `N=20,000`; vary `M` independently (20k, 100k and larger only after resource admission). Include bounded K≈32/128/512, clustered hotspots, thin walls/corridors, coincident points, highly unequal radii, all-visible, empty results, negative coordinates, extreme finite magnitudes, radius zero, boundary and nextafter cases. Vary center coherence independently of object coherence and include centers not present in the point set. Do not cap exact results to manufacture bounded K: construct density/radius fixtures and measure the realized distribution, including p99/max K.

Correctness gates: exact per-query sorted ID equality with the current brute-force oracle; no omissions, duplicates or narrowing of IDs; duplicate-ID upsert, remove/reinsert, invalid-input atomicity; current mutations visible; scalar/SIMD agreement; preserved extreme-distance behavior. Include operation traces and metamorphic translation/permutation tests only where the floating-point contract predicts invariance (arbitrary translation need not preserve rounded arithmetic). For parallel epochs test no mixed-epoch rows, ownership transfer and bounded reclamation; that server snapshot API must not weaken the existing immediate-query API.

Performance gates: predeclare fixtures, compiler/flags/binary hashes, preparation policy, seeds, output mode and thread count. Retain full evidence required by the repository's existing simultaneous comparison gate; an exploratory sample or a 2k-to-20k linear extrapolation cannot pass it. Record update/refit/rebuild, query search, output expansion, membership diff, serialization, queue wait, send and client receive separately, plus end-to-end latency, CPU time, peak RSS, allocations, candidates/tests, returned bytes, packets/s, egress, missed ticks and replica age. Preserve time series or bounded histograms for tails and counters; avoid saving every pair for every frame. Histograms have a tunable bounded footprint ([HdrHistogram](https://github.com/HdrHistogram/HdrHistogram)).

Final measurements must be serial and repeated with randomized/interleaved variant order. Record thermal/power state and background activity; do not pause or kill active workers to obtain isolation. On this shared machine, mark measurements exploratory; use a suitable isolated host later. LLVM notes that low noise does not eliminate bias, while Google Benchmark provides repetitions, warmup and interleaving mechanisms ([LLVM](https://llvm.org/docs/Benchmarking.html), [Google Benchmark](https://github.com/google/benchmark/blob/main/docs/user_guide.md)).

A complete server test needs independent clients/load generators, 20,000 established authenticated connections, scheduled input load, declared packet loss/RTT/jitter, moving entities, receiving/reconstruction checks and a sustained interval long enough to reveal queue/baseline accumulation. Measure latency from intended schedule time; a client that waits for replies before creating more work can hide overload. wrk2 documents this coordinated-omission issue; its HTTP implementation is a measurement reference, not a drop-in game load generator ([wrk2](https://github.com/giltene/wrk2)). Track offered versus completed load and rejected/admitted work explicitly.

## Decision

First pursue coherent cell/packed and dual-tree CPU variants with true u64 reporting, then combine the winner with refit and replication sharing. A bounded-K 20k service is a plausible engineering target under an explicit machine, simulation and egress budget; the current evidence does not establish it. Full-fidelity all-to-all at 20 Hz has terabit-scale payload requirements in the illustrative 32-byte scenario and needs a different declared service policy. Existing 2,000-query screening times identify kernels worth investigating, but are neither a scaling law nor connected-server evidence.

## Follow-up: geometry and workload hints

[AQWA](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/aref/papers/aqwa2015twoQueues.pdf)
provides primary evidence that point distribution alone need not determine the
best spatial partition. Its adaptive Hadoop partitioner uses query-workload
statistics; its results do not establish a CPU-tree optimization here.
[SciPy's construction options](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.spatial.cKDTree.html)
also make leaf size, compact data bounds and median splitting explicit cost
choices rather than universal constants.

Our new radius-aware candidate is a separate hypothesis: when a global dimension
is narrower than the configured cell-size hint, retain its exact bounds/filtering
but prefer median splits along wider dimensions. All-axis fallback handles worlds
smaller than the hint in every dimension. This may reduce duplicated horizontal
traversal in thin 3D worlds; small-radius or off-slab queries may instead lose
pruning efficiency. The hint never restricts accepted radii or drops coordinates.
It requires full-dimensional membership proofs, thin 2D/3D edge tests, lifecycle
cost accounting and comparison against the unchanged topology on registered and
held-out inputs. It is not claimed as an implementation of AQWA.
